What happens after the door closes

40 to 80%

of what is said in a medical appointment is forgotten almost straight away. Almost half of what is remembered is wrong.

Kessels (2003), Patients' memory for medical information, J R Soc Med

“…and if the cough is still there in ten days, ring us…”

So people piece it together on the way home. They ring the surgery to check. They tell their daughter the half they kept hold of. Your joint report with the RCGP described the NHS as a maze. A lot of that maze is made of things people were told once, in a small room, at a stressful moment.

This is already happening

7 in 10 patients would like to record their appointments. About half would do it without asking.

Elwyn, Barr and Grande (2015), Patients recording clinical encounters, BMJ Open

Patients are already recording, often quietly, on whatever is in their pocket. The real choice is whether that happens in the open, safely, with the patient firmly in charge. MyScribe is built for that version.

Your October webinar asked what meaningful consent looks like in practice, for patients and for the professionals sitting opposite them.Patient consent: making sure your voice is heard, understood and respected

Consent works both ways in a consultation. The patient is asked to agree to care, and they deserve the chance to hold on to what they agreed to. MyScribe makes consent the very first screen, and gives the patient their own record of what was said, in their own hands.

On AI, and being told

Your research found that only 1 in 10 patients who believed AI was used in their care had been clearly told.

MyScribe starts the other way round. The patient asks, out loud, before anything is recorded. If the answer is no, the phone goes away.

Most of the AI now arriving in consulting rooms works for the clinician and the organisation. MyScribe works for the person on the other side of the desk. We see the two as complementary: the clinician keeps their notes, and the patient finally keeps theirs.

Measured against your six principles

We read the Six Principles of Patient Partnership as a design brief.

1

Treating patients as equals

Both people in the room leave with a record of the same conversation. The patient's is theirs alone.

2

Patients who are fully informed

A plain words summary, read aloud on request, with every sentence linked back to the moment it was said.

3

Shared decision making

Decisions keep going after the appointment ends. People can go back, check, and talk it through at home with the people they trust.

4

Recognising inequalities

Nothing to learn, read aloud for people who prefer to listen, free to download, and Gifted Minutes so cost never decides who gets to remember.

5

Seeking patient input

Our Patient Recording Standard is an open draft, published under CC BY 4.0, written to be challenged and rewritten.

6

Joining services around patients

Fewer calls back to the surgery just to check what was said, and one shareable account of each conversation as people move between services.

What we hold ourselves to

We add nothing to what was said, and do everything with it.

Ask first

Recording begins with an open question, asked out loud.

Audio is deleted once it is written down

The words and the summary stay, encrypted, in the person's account until they delete them.

Exact words where precision matters

Medicine names and doses stay out of the summary. Ask Your Conversation takes people straight to the exact words, with unclear wording highlighted.

The record belongs to the person

Sold to nobody. Used to train no AI. Shared only with the people they choose.

For the people who couldn't be in the room

Carers hear what was actually said, rather than what was half remembered on the way home.

You will know from your years with the Dementia Action Alliance how much rests on the person beside the patient. Today, people share a summary with whoever helps them look after their health. The person receiving it needs no app. Next comes trusted people: a carer who can read Dad's summary, see the full conversation if he chooses, or record on his behalf with his permission.

Fair by design

Pennies, not pounds. And a way in for everyone.

Free to download, first conversation free, then a little credit topped up when needed. Nobody is ever asked to pay before or during a conversation.

Gifted Minutes lets people and organisations buy minutes for those who need them. Using them takes one tap, with no forms and no proof of need, and nobody who gifts minutes ever sees a conversation. A charity could gift minutes to the people it supports, including the people who ring a helpline because they came away from an appointment unsure what was said.

The Patient Recording Standard

A standard with “patient” in its name should be written mostly by patients.

We wrote the first draft while building MyScribe, and it measures any recording tool, ours included. Early challenges from patients and carers have already pushed it further, especially on carers' rights and on who gets the final say. We would love the Patients Association's members to take it from here.
